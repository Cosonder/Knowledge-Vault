# Definition

The **lexicon**（词库，词汇，lexique）is the complete inventory of [[Word 词 mot|words]] and other lexical units (morphemes, fixed expressions) of a language, together with the information speakers know about each: pronunciation, meaning, grammatical category, gender, irregular forms, typical combinations.

The lexicon can mean (1) the vocabulary of a language as a whole, or (2) the **mental lexicon**（心理词库）of an individual speaker. The study of the lexicon is [[Lexicology 词汇学 lexicologie|lexicology]]; recording it in dictionaries is lexicography（词典学，lexicographie）.

In French, *lexique* (the system of a language) is often distinguished from *vocabulaire* (the words actually used in a text or by a person).

# What a lexical entry contains (French example)

> **cheval** /ʃ(ə)val/, n.m., pl. **chevaux** — horse 马
> **cheval** contains: phonological form, category (noun), gender (masculine), irregular plural, meaning, and collocations such as *monter à cheval* 骑马, *à cheval sur* 横跨.

Information that is unpredictable must be stored in the lexicon:
* gender of nouns: *le* livre / *la* livre (different words!) — see [[Gender of nouns 名词的性 genre des noms|Gender of nouns]]
* irregular forms: *œil* → *yeux*; *aller* → *j'irai*
* *h aspiré* vs *h muet*: *le héros* vs *l'héroïne* — see [[Elision 省音 élision|elision]]
* auxiliary choice: *Il **est** venu* vs *Il **a** mangé* — see [[Perfect 复合过去时 passé composé|passé composé]]
* idioms: *poser un lapin* 放鸽子 — see [[Phraseology 熟语学 phraséologie|phraseology]]

# Structure of the French lexicon

By origin (see [[Etymology 语源学 étymologie|etymology]]):
* **Inherited words** (mots héréditaires) from popular Latin, heavily changed: *eau* < *aqua*, *père* < *patrem*.
* **Learned words** (mots savants) borrowed later from classical Latin/Greek, close to the source: *aquatique*, *paternel*.
* **Borrowings** (emprunts): from Frankish (*guerre, jardin*), Italian (*banque, balcon*), Arabic (*algèbre, café*), English (*week-end, parking*).

This explains doublets（同源异形词，doublets）such as:
> **frêle** / **fragile** (both < *fragilis*)
> **hôtel** / **hôpital** (both < *hospitale*)

# Remarks

1. The lexicon is an open system: new words enter (*courriel*, *ubériser*), others fall out of use. Grammar, by contrast, changes slowly.
2. Since English borrowed massively from French after 1066, much of the English learned lexicon is French-derived, which helps recognition but also creates false friends; see [[Cognate 同源词 cognat|cognate]] and [[A Comparison of French Words with English Words 法英词汇对比 comparaison franco-anglaise|A Comparison of French Words with English Words]].
