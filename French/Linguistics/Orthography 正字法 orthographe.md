# Definition

An **orthography** is a set of conventions for writing a language, including norms of spelling, punctuation, word boundaries, capitalization, hyphenation, and emphasis. (Source: [Wikipedia — Orthography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthography))

正字法是一种语言书写的规范体系，包括拼写、标点、分词（词的边界）、大小写、连字符和强调方式等。在法语中 *orthographe* 一般就指“拼写”，*faire des fautes d'orthographe* = 拼写错误。

The basic unit of writing is the **grapheme**（字位，graphème），the written counterpart of the [[Phoneme 音位 phonème|phoneme]]; see [[Emic units 位单位 unités émiques|Emic units]]. For the specifics of French (alphabet, diacritics, ligatures), see [[French orthography 法语正字法 orthographe française|French orthography]] and [[Alphabet 字母表 alphabet|Alphabet]].

# Classification (types of writing systems)

1. **Alphabetic**（字母文字）: graphemes ≈ phonemes — Latin, Greek, Cyrillic.
2. **Abjad**（辅音音素文字）: mainly consonants — Arabic, Hebrew.
3. **Syllabic**（音节文字）: one sign per syllable — Japanese kana.
4. **Logographic / morphosyllabic**（语素文字）: one sign per morpheme — Chinese characters.

Within alphabetic systems, orthographies are **shallow**（浅层，transparent）when spelling follows pronunciation closely (Spanish, Italian, Finnish) and **deep**（深层，opaque）when it does not (English, French).

# French orthography: deep in one direction

French is **hard to spell but fairly easy to read**: from spelling to sound the rules are regular, but from sound to spelling there are many choices.

One phoneme, many spellings:
> /o/ : m**o**t, h**ô**pital, ch**au**d, b**eau**
> /ɛ̃/ : v**in**, p**ain**, pl**ein**, f**aim**, v**ingt**, th**ym**

Homophones distinguished only in writing:
> **vers, vert, verre, ver, vair** — all /vɛʁ/
> **cent, sang, sans, s'en, sent** — all /sɑ̃/

Silent letters with grammatical or etymological roles:
> grammatical: ils parl**ent** /paʁl/, petit**e** /pətit/ vs petit /pəti/
> etymological: doi**g**t, tem**ps** — see [[Etymology 语源学 étymologie|etymology]]

Orthography also encodes phonological processes such as [[Elision 省音 élision|elision]] (the apostrophe: *l'ami*) and the euphonic *-t-* that avoids [[Hiatus 元音连续 hiatus|hiatus]] (*a-t-il*).

# Reforms

The *Rectifications de l'orthographe* (1990) allowed, among others: *ognon* (for *oignon*), *nénufar*, no circumflex on *i/u* where it has no distinctive role (*connaitre*, *paraitre*, but still *dû*, *sûr*), and systematic hyphens in compound numerals (*vingt-et-un*, *deux-cents*). Both old and new spellings are accepted.

# Contrast

* English is also deep but irregular in both directions (*though, through, tough*); French is regular when reading.
* Chinese writing is not phonographic: 正字法 concerns correct character forms（如“戊/戌/戍”）、简繁体规范，而拼音是辅助的拼写系统。
