# Definition

Lexicology is the branch of linguistics that concerns the words of a specific language.
A word is the smallest meaningful unit of a language that can stand on its own.
A word might have one or more small components called morphemes.

**Lexicology**（词汇学，lexicologie）studies the [[Lexicon 词库 lexique|lexicon]]: the form, meaning, origin, structure and use of [[Word 词 mot|words]] and other lexical units. It sits at the crossroads of [[Morphology 构词学 morphologie|morphology]] (word structure), [[Semantics 语义学 sémantique|semantics]] (word meaning) and [[Etymology 语源学 étymologie|etymology]] (word history).

Its applied sister discipline is **lexicography**（词典学，lexicographie）, the making of dictionaries (*Le Petit Robert*, *Larousse*, *TLFi*).

![[Lexeme 词位 lexème]]

A **lexeme**（词位，lexème）is the abstract unit of the lexicon that groups all the inflected forms of a word: *va, allons, irai, allé* belong to the lexeme ALLER. Its citation form is the **lemma**（词目，lemme）, normally the infinitive for verbs and the masculine singular for nouns and adjectives. (In French linguistics, *lexème* sometimes means only the lexical root, as opposed to grammatical morphemes.)

# Main topics

1. **Word formation**（构词，formation des mots）: derivation, compounding, conversion. See [[Morphology 构词学 morphologie|morphology]].
> *heureux* → *mal**heureux*** → *malheureuse**ment***
2. **Lexical semantics**（词汇语义）: sense relations between words.
	* Synonymy 同义: *commencer* / *débuter*
	* Antonymy 反义: *chaud* / *froid*
	* Hyponymy 上下义: *rose* ⊂ *fleur*
	* Polysemy 一词多义: *une **feuille*** = leaf / sheet of paper
	* Homonymy 同音/同形异义: ***vers*** (toward) / ***vers*** (line of verse) / ***ver*** (worm) / ***verre*** (glass) / ***vert*** (green) — all /vɛʁ/
3. **Etymology and borrowing**. See [[Etymology 语源学 étymologie|etymology]], [[Cognate 同源词 cognat|cognate]].
4. **Multi-word units**: idioms, collocations. See [[Phraseology 熟语学 phraséologie|phraseology]].
5. **Lexical variation**: register (*voiture* / *bagnole*), region (*septante* in Belgium/Switzerland vs *soixante-dix*), neologisms (*courriel*).

# Remarks

1. French has an exceptionally high number of homophones because of silent letters, which makes homonymy a central practical problem for learners and for French [[Orthography 正字法 orthographe|orthography]].
2. 汉语词汇学传统上重视“字”与“词”的关系、双音化趋势和成语；法语词汇学则更关注拉丁语源、派生系统和借词。
