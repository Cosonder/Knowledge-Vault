## Name originality

The name **Standard & Poor's 500** (commonly abbreviated as the **S&P 500**) sounds unusual to those outside the financial world because it isn't named after a financial concept. Instead, it is a combination of the surnames of two historical figures in financial publishing, plus the number of companies included in the index.
Here is the breakdown of how the name came to be:
### 1. The "Poor" Part: Henry William Poor
In 1860, a man named **Henry William Poor** published a book called *History of Railroads and Canals in the United States*.
 * At the time, the railway industry was the "tech boom" of the 19th century, but information about these companies was sparse and unreliable.
 * Poor’s book provided investors with much-needed, unbiased financial and operational data about railway companies.
 * He later co-founded **H.V. and H.W. Poor Co.** with his son, continuing to publish authoritative investment guides.
### 2. The "Standard" Part: The Standard Statistics Bureau
In 1906, another corporate entity called the **Standard Statistics Bureau** (later known as the Standard Statistics Company) was formed.
 * While Poor’s company focused heavily on corporate history and structural data, Standard Statistics focused on providing **sovereign, municipal, and corporate bond ratings**, as well as financial statistics.
 * In 1923, Standard Statistics created the first incarnation of the stock market index we know today, though it originally tracked only 233 American companies and updated on a weekly basis.
### 3. The Merger: Standard & Poor's (1941)
In 1941, the two major financial publishing competitors decided to join forces. H.V. and H.W. Poor Co. merged with the Standard Statistics Company to create a financial data powerhouse named **Standard & Poor's Corp.**
### 4. The "500" Part (1957)
On **March 4, 1957**, the newly merged Standard & Poor's expanded its existing stock index to track **500 of the largest publicly traded American companies**. This was a technological milestone, as it was the first index to be calculated mathematically in real-time using early IBM computer systems.
Thus, the **Standard & Poor's 500** was born—representing the company that created it (Standard & Poor's) and the exact number of market-leading stocks it tracks (500).

> 📌 **Fun Fact:** Today, the index is maintained by a joint-venture company called **S&P Dow Jones Indices** (which also owns the famous Dow Jones Industrial Average). Standard & Poor's itself operates as a division of the financial intelligence giant **S&P Global**.
