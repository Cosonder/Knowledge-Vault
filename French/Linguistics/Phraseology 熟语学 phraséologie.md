# Definition

**Phraseology**（熟语学，短语学，phraséologie）is the study of **fixed or semi-fixed multi-word expressions**: combinations of [[Word 词 mot|words]] that are stored in the [[Lexicon 词库 lexique|lexicon]] as units rather than freely built by grammar. It is a subfield of [[Lexicology 词汇学 lexicologie|lexicology]].

Such units are called *phraséologismes*, *expressions figées*, *locutions*. Their typical properties:
* **Fixedness**（凝固性，figement）: limited substitution or modification.
* **Non-compositionality**（非组合性）: the meaning is not (fully) the sum of the parts — see [[Semantics 语义学 sémantique|semantics]].
* **Institutionalization**: recognized and reused by the speech community.

# Classification

1. **Idioms**（习语，expressions idiomatiques）— opaque meaning.
> **poser un lapin** à qqn 放某人鸽子（字面：放一只兔子）
> **coûter les yeux de la tête** 贵得要命（字面：值头上的眼睛）
> **avoir le cafard** 情绪低落（字面：有蟑螂）
> **tomber dans les pommes** 晕倒
2. **Collocations**（搭配，collocations）— transparent but conventional combinations; the learner must memorize which word goes with which.
> **prendre** une décision (English *make* a decision)
> **faire** une promenade (English *take* a walk)
> une pluie **battante** 倾盆大雨 (English *heavy* rain)
> **célibataire endurci** 铁杆单身汉
3. **Compound function words / locutions grammaticales** — fixed units acting as one grammatical word.
> **à cause de**, **grâce à**, **au lieu de**, **bien que**, **tout à coup**, **il y a**
4. **Proverbs and sayings**（谚语，proverbes / dictons）— complete sentences.
> **Petit à petit, l'oiseau fait son nid.** 积少成多。
> **Qui vivra verra.** 走着瞧 / 日久自明。
5. **Routine formulas**（套语，formules de routine）— pragmatic expressions.
> **Bon appétit !** / **À tes souhaits !**（对打喷嚏的人说）/ **Ça marche !**

# Tests of fixedness

> Il a **cassé sa pipe**. (= il est mort)
> ? Il a cassé **ses pipes** / **sa grande pipe**. — only the literal meaning remains

# Contrast with Chinese and English

* Chinese 成语（chengyu）are an extreme form of phraseology: four characters, often from classical texts（守株待兔、画蛇添足）. French equivalents tend to be longer and more colloquial.
* The same image may be used differently: French *avoir **un chat** dans la gorge* = English *have **a frog** in one's throat* 嗓子哑了.
* Collocations are the main source of "correct but unnatural" learner French: \**faire une décision* is understandable but wrong.
