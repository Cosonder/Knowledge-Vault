# Introduction

The subject (*le sujet*) is the grammatical function of the element that the verb agrees with in [[Person 人称 personne|person]] and number. Semantically it is often the agent (who does the action), but not always: in the passive or with *être*, it is what the sentence is about.

> **Les enfants** jouent.
> **La lettre** a été écrite par Paul. (passive: subject ≠ agent)

Test: the subject answers *qui est-ce qui ? / qu'est-ce qui ?* + verb, and can be framed by *c'est … qui*.

> *C'est **Paul** qui* a écrit la lettre.

| [[Personal pronoun 人称代词 pronom personnel\|personal pronoun]] (clitic) | **Il** pleut. **Nous** partons.    |

| subordinate clause                           | **Qu'il soit en retard** m'étonne.              |

# Agreement with the subject

The verb agrees in person and number; with *être* (and passive, *être*-verbs), the [[Past participle 过去分词 participe passé|past participle]] and attribute agree in gender and number. See [[Agreement 配合 accord|agreement]].

> **Elles** sont parti**es**. **Ma sœur** est contente.

Special cases:

- Several subjects → plural: *Paul et Marie **sont** venus.*
- Different persons: the 1st person prevails over the 2nd, the 2nd over the 3rd: *Toi et moi, **nous** partirons.*
- Collective noun + complement: singular or plural according to meaning: *Une foule de gens **attendait / attendaient**.*
- *c'est / ce sont*: *Ce **sont** mes amis.*
- *qui* relative: the verb agrees with the antecedent: *C'est moi qui **suis** là.*

# Position

Normally **before** the verb (subject–verb–object order). The subject is placed after the verb in [[Inversion 倒装 inversion|inversion]]:

1. Questions: ***Viens-tu*** ? ***Où va*** *ton frère* ?
2. Incises: *« Oui », **dit-il**.*
3. After some adverbs at the head of the sentence (*peut-être, sans doute, ainsi, aussi* = therefore): ***Peut-être viendra-t-il.***
4. Stylistic inversion in relative and other subordinate clauses: *la maison où **habitait mon grand-père***.

See also [[Interrogative structure 疑问结构 structure interrogative|interrogative structure]].

# The impersonal subject *il*

French requires an overt subject. With impersonal verbs, *il* is a dummy subject with no reference (English *it / there*).

> **Il** pleut. **Il** faut partir. **Il** y a un problème.
> **Il** est arrivé un accident. (real subject: *un accident*)

In such constructions, the verb agrees with *il* (3rd person singular): *Il est arrivé **des** accidents.*

# Comparison with English

- Both languages require an explicit subject (unlike Spanish or Italian).
- French clitic subject pronouns cannot be stressed or separated from the verb; for emphasis, a tonic pronoun is added: ***Moi**, je* pense que…
- French inverts the subject more freely (after adverbs, in relative clauses).
