# Definition

**Semiotics**（符号学，sémiotique / sémiologie）is the study of **signs** and sign systems: anything that stands for something else to someone. Language is the most complex sign system, but semiotics also covers gestures, traffic lights, images, fashion, music and rituals. Linguistics can thus be seen as one branch of semiotics, and [[Semantics 语义学 sémantique|semantics]] as the part concerned with linguistic meaning.

Two founding traditions:
* **Ferdinand de Saussure** (Swiss, *Cours de linguistique générale*, 1916) proposed *sémiologie*, "a science that studies the life of signs within social life".
* **Charles S. Peirce** (American) developed *semiotics* with a triadic model of the sign.

# Saussure's linguistic sign

A sign（符号，signe）is the union of:
* **signifier**（能指，signifiant）: the sound image, e.g. /ʃa/
* **signified**（所指，signifié）: the concept "cat"

> ***chat*** = signifiant /ʃa/ + signifié「猫」

Key principles:
1. **Arbitrariness**（任意性，arbitraire du signe）: no natural link between sound and meaning — French ***arbre***, English *tree*, Chinese 树 all name the same concept.
2. **Linearity**（线条性）: signifiers unfold in time, one after another.
3. **Value by difference**（价值）: a sign's meaning comes from its contrasts within the system. French ***mouton*** covers both the animal and the meat, whereas English splits it into *sheep* / *mutton*, so the two words do not have the same value.
4. ***Langue* vs *parole***（语言 / 言语）: the shared system vs individual acts of speech. This distinction underlies the [[Phoneme 音位 phonème|phoneme]] (langue) vs [[Phone 音素 phone|phone]] (parole) distinction.

# Peirce's types of signs

| Type | Relation to object | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Icon**（像似符号，icône） | resemblance | a portrait, onomatopoeia *cocorico* 喔喔喔 |
| **Index**（指示符号，indice） | causal / contiguous link | smoke → fire; *ici, maintenant, je* |
| **Symbol**（规约符号，symbole） | convention | most words, a red light |

# Remarks

1. Onomatopoeia shows that even "iconic" words are partly conventional: a rooster says *cocorico* in French, *cock-a-doodle-doo* in English, 喔喔喔 in Chinese.
2. Chinese characters are an interesting semiotic case: some originated as icons (象形 日、山), but in modern use they function as symbols.
3. Later French semioticians include Roland Barthes (*Mythologies*), A. J. Greimas and Claude Lévi-Strauss, whose "mytheme" is one of the [[Emic units 位单位 unités émiques|Emic units]].
