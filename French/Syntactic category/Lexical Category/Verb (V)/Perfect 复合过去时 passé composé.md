## Agreement
Past participle may agree in gender and number with the subject or the object of the verb, in the cases detailed below. Agreement of past participles with pronominal verbs is treated in [[Pronominal verb 代动词 verbe pronominal|pronominal verb]]. 

The past participle of those verbs with "être" as their auxiliary agrees with the subject.

The past participle of those verbs with "avoir" as their auxiliary agrees with the **direct object** of the verb, but only if this direct object appears **before the verb** in the sentence.

Hence if the direct object appears **after the verb**, there is no agreement, i.e., we use the default form of the past participle, the masculine singular form.

There are four major situations when the COD appears before the verb.
1. Object pronoun.

> J'ai vu **Marie**.
> Je <strong>l'</strong>ai **vue**.

> J'ai acheté **les fleurs**.
> Je **les** ai **achetées**.
2. Relative **que**.
>J'ai acheté **les fleurs**.
> les fleurs que j'ai **achetées**

> J'ai écrit **la lettre**.
> la lettre **que** j'ai **écrite**
3. Interrogative
> Tu as acheté une robe.
> Quelle robe as-tu achetée?
4. Exclamation
> J'ai fait **une erreur**.
> **Quelle erreur** j'ai faite !

Also note that a COI does not trigger agreement no matter where it is.

> J'ai parlé à Marie.  
> Je **lui** ai **parlé**.

### No agreement

The past participle does not agree in the following cases:
1. With other complements (indirect object, adverbs, etc.)
> la tante à qui j'ai **écrit**
> l'année où j'ai **écrit** un livre

2. When the subject is the impersonal *il* 
> les difficultés qu'il y a **eu**
> ❌ _les difficultés qu'il y a eues_
> Quelle chaleur il a **fait**!
