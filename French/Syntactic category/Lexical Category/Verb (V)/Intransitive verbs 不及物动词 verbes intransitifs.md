# Introduction

An intransitive verb (*verbe intransitif*) has **no object** ([[Object 宾语 objet|object]]), neither direct (COD) nor indirect (COI). The action does not "pass over" to a complement. It can still take circumstantial complements (place, time, manner).

> Il **dort**.
> Elle **est partie** à huit heures. (*à huit heures* is a circumstantial complement, not an object)

Contrast:

| type                        | construction            | example                     |
| --------------------------- | ----------------------- | --------------------------- |
| transitive direct           | verb + COD              | Je **regarde** la télé.     |
| transitive indirect         | verb + preposition + COI | Je **parle à** Marie.      |
| intransitive                | verb alone              | Je **marche**.              |

注意：法语语法传统上把 *parler à qn, obéir à qn* 这类“间接及物”与真正的不及物动词区分开；英语语法常把它们一并称为 intransitive。

# Common intransitive verbs

*aller, venir, arriver, partir, naître, mourir, dormir, marcher, courir, nager, rire, pleurer, tomber, rester, exister, briller, éternuer, voyager, vieillir, grandir, réussir*

# Auxiliary in compound tenses

Most intransitive verbs take *avoir*: *j'ai dormi, il a ri, elle a voyagé*.

A small closed group of intransitive verbs of **movement or change of state** take *être* (see [[Perfect 复合过去时 Passé composé|passé composé]]). Mnemonic: **DR & MRS VANDERTRAMP**.

| verb              | past participle | verb              | past participle |
| ----------------- | --------------- | ----------------- | --------------- |
| aller             | allé            | venir (devenir, revenir) | venu      |
| arriver           | arrivé          | partir            | parti           |
| entrer (rentrer)  | entré           | sortir            | sorti           |
| monter            | monté           | descendre         | descendu        |
| naître            | né              | mourir            | mort            |
| rester            | resté           | tomber            | tombé           |
| retourner         | retourné        | passer            | passé           |

With *être*, the [[Past participle 过去分词 Participe passé|past participle]] [[Agreement 配合 accord|agrees]] with the subject:

> Elles **sont arrivées** hier.

# Verbs that are both transitive and intransitive

Some verbs of the *être* group become transitive (with a COD) and then take **avoir**.

> Elle **est montée** au premier étage. (intransitive)
> Elle **a monté** les valises. (transitive: *les valises* is COD)
> -> She went upstairs. / She carried the suitcases up.

> Je **suis sorti** hier soir. / J'**ai sorti** le chien.
> Il **est passé** me voir. / Il **a passé** un examen.
> Nous **sommes descendus**. / Nous **avons descendu** l'escalier.

Other verbs change meaning with transitivity:

> Le bois **brûle**. (intransitive) / Il **brûle** des papiers. (transitive)
> Il **mange**. (absolute use of a transitive verb: the object is implied, not absent from the verb's valency)

# Intransitive verbs and the passive

An intransitive verb has no COD, so it **cannot be put in the passive** [[Voice 语态 voix|voice]]: *✗ Il a été dormi.* French uses the impersonal or *on* instead.

# Impersonal use

Many intransitive verbs can be used with impersonal *il*, the real subject following:

> **Il est arrivé** un accident.
> -> An accident happened.

# English equivalent

Largely the same notion (*sleep, arrive, die*), but English verbs often allow both uses more freely (*I walked / I walked the dog* ~ *j'ai promené le chien*).
