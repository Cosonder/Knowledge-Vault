## What's the relationship between NASDAQ and ETF?
The relationship between the **NASDAQ** and **ETFs (Exchange-Traded Funds)** is best understood as a mix of **marketplace, partnership, and individual products**.

They are entirely different concepts, but they cross paths constantly in the stock market. Here is a breakdown of how they connect:

### 1. The NASDAQ serves as the "Marketplace" for ETFs

The NASDAQ is a physical and digital **Stock Exchange** (just like the New York Stock Exchange).

- ETFs are securities that trade like individual stocks. Therefore, fund managers (like BlackRock, Vanguard, or Invesco) list their ETFs on the NASDAQ exchange.
    
- When you buy or sell an ETF during market hours, your trade is very likely being processed through NASDAQ's electronic trading systems.
    

### 2. NASDAQ designs the "Blueprints" (Indices) that ETFs copy

Most ETFs are **passive**, meaning they don't try to guess which stocks will win. Instead, they choose a specific index (a list of stocks) and copy it exactly. NASDAQ is one of the world's largest creators of these indices.

- **The Index (The Blueprint):** NASDAQ creates the mathematical list of companies, such as the **NASDAQ-100 Index** (the 100 largest non-financial companies listed on its exchange).
    
- **The ETF (The Product):** An investment company takes that blueprint and builds an ETF. For example, Invesco created the famous **QQQ ETF**, which buys the exact stocks in the NASDAQ-100.
    

### 3. Comparing a NASDAQ ETF vs. Other ETFs

When everyday investors talk about the "relationship" between the two, they are usually deciding whether to buy a **NASDAQ-tracking ETF** or a different type of ETF, like an **S&P 500 ETF**.

The difference comes down to the flavor of the investment:

|**Feature**|**NASDAQ-100 ETF (e.g., QQQ)**|**S&P 500 ETF (e.g., VOO or SPY)**|
|---|---|---|
|**What it tracks**|The 100 largest non-financial companies on the NASDAQ exchange.|The 500 largest profitable companies across _all_ US exchanges.|
|**Industry Bias**|**Heavy Tech & Innovation Focus.** (Apple, Microsoft, NVIDIA, Amazon, Alphabet).|**Well-Rounded Diversification.** Includes Tech, but also Banking, Energy, and Healthcare.|
|**Risk Profile**|**Aggressive / High Growth.** Aggressive gains when tech booms; steeper losses when tech crashes.|**Balanced / Moderate.** Steady long-term growth, smoother rides during market downturns.|

> 💡 **Summary**
> 
> Think of the **NASDAQ** as both a **giant digital mall** and a **brand catalog**. An **ETF** is a **gift basket** you can buy inside that mall.
> 
> You can buy a gift basket _packed exclusively with NASDAQ's tech-heavy catalog_ (a NASDAQ ETF), or you can browse the NASDAQ mall to buy a mixed basket containing items from all over the country (an S&P 500 ETF).
