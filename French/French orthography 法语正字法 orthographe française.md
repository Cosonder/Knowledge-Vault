French orthography encompasses the spelling and punctuation of the French language.

法语正字法（*orthographe française*）是法语的拼写与标点规范。其特点是：拼写高度依赖词源（大量不发音字母反映拉丁语来源，见 [[Etymology 语源学 étymologie|etymology]]），从拼写到读音较为规则，但从读音到拼写往往不唯一（*vert, verre, vers, ver, vair* 都读 [vɛʁ]）。General concept: [[Orthography 正字法 orthographe|orthography]].

# [[Alphabet 字母表 alphabet|Alphabet]]

French uses the 26 letters of the Latin alphabet, plus letters with diacritics and two ligatures. *k* and *w* appear almost only in borrowed words (*kilo, wagon*).

Accents in French do not mark stress (unlike Spanish). They mark pronunciation, distinguish homographs, or keep a trace of etymology. Capital letters should keep their accents (*État, À bientôt*).

## Acute accent

Only on **é**, pronounced closed [e].
> **é**t**é**, caf**é**, parl**é** (past participle of 1st group)

Often corresponds to an old *s* before a consonant (é- ↔ English *s-*): *école* (school), *étudiant* (student), *état* (state). See [[Cognate 同源词 cognat|cognate]].

## Grave accent

- **è**: open [ɛ]: *mère, très, après*; also in conjugation (*je lève, j'achète*, see [[Conjugation 变位 conjugaison|conjugation]]).
- **à, ù**: no change in pronunciation, distinguishes homographs:

| without | with |
| ------- | ---- |
| a (has) | à (to, at) |
| la (the) | là (there) |
| ou (or) | où (where) |

## Circumflex

On **â, ê, î, ô, û**. Often marks a lost *s* (English cognate keeps it):
> for**ê**t (forest), h**ô**pital (hospital), f**ê**te (feast), ch**â**teau (castle), **î**le (isle)

Effects:
- *ê* = open [ɛ]; *ô* = closed [o]; *â* may be back [ɑ] (traditional).
- distinguishes homographs: *du / dû, sur / sûr, mur / mûr*.
- the 1990 spelling reform allows dropping it on *i, u* when it has no distinguishing role (*connaitre, ile, cout*), but traditional spelling is still common.

## Diaeresis

Indicates that a vowel is pronounced separately from the preceding one (no digraph), see [[Hiatus 元音连续 hiatus|hiatus]]:
> na**ï**f [naif] (vs *ai* [ɛ]), No**ë**l, ma**ï**s
> aig**uë** (the *u* is pronounced: [egy])

## Cedilla

Only **ç**, before *a, o, u*, indicates soft [s] instead of [k]:
> fa**ç**ade, gar**ç**on, re**ç**u, nous commen**ç**ons
Before *e, i*, plain *c* is already [s] (*ceci*), so no cedilla.

# Ligatures
合字，合体字

| Ligature | Name                         | Pronunciation | Example                     |
| -------- | ---------------------------- | ------------- | --------------------------- |
| œ        | *e dans l'o* (o-e lié)        | [œ], [ø], [e]  | cœur, sœur, œil, œuvre, œdème |
| æ        | *e dans l'a* (a-e lié)        | [e]           | ex æquo, curriculum vitæ     |

Mac input: option + q → œ, option + ' (apostrophe) → æ (layout-dependent). Writing *oe* instead of *œ* is tolerated but not standard.

# Punctuation

Main differences from English:
- A non-breaking space precedes the double signs **; : ! ?** and is used inside guillemets: *Quoi ? « Bonjour ! »*
- Quotation marks are guillemets **« »**.
- Decimal comma, space for thousands: *3,14* ; *1 000 000*.
- Days, months, languages, nationality adjectives are lowercase: *lundi, janvier, le français, un livre français* (but *un Français*, the person).

# Remarks

1. Final consonants are often silent: *petit, grand, temps*. The mnemonic **CaReFuL**: final *c, r, f, l* are usually pronounced (*sac, finir, neuf, sel*), with exceptions (*-er* verbs).
2. Silent final consonants may be pronounced in [[Liaison 联诵 liaison|liaison]].
3. *h muet* vs *h aspiré* is not visible in spelling but governs elision and liaison: *l'homme* vs *le héros*.
