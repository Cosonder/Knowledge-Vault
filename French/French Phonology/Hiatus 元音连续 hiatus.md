总结避免hiatus的所有情况

# Definition

A **hiatus**（元音连续，元音碰撞，hiatus）is the meeting of two vowels belonging to **two different syllables**, with no consonant between them. It is opposed to a diphthong, where two vowel qualities are in the same syllable.

> within a word: *po.**è**te* /pɔ.ɛt/, *ha.**ï**r* /a.iʁ/, *cré.**er*** /kʁe.e/
> across words: *il va **à** Paris* /il va a paʁi/, *tu **as** eu* /ty a y/

French tolerates hiatus inside words (the *tréma* often marks it in writing: *naïf*, *maïs*, *Noël*), but at word boundaries the language has developed many devices to avoid it, especially in grammatical words. 法语追求音节 CV 结构的流畅性（euphonie），所以大量“特殊形式”其实都是为了避免 hiatus。

Within a word, hiatus is kept (*pays* /pe.i/) or softened in speech by a transitional glide (*crier* \[kʁi.je\]).

# Devices for avoiding hiatus

## 1. Elision
A final vowel is dropped and replaced by an apostrophe. See [[Elision 省音 élision|elision]].
> **l'**ami, **j'**arrive, **qu'**il vienne, **s'**il pleut

## 2. Liaison
A latent final consonant is pronounced before a vowel. See [[Liaison 联诵 liaison|liaison]].
> les‿amis /le.z‿a.mi/, ils‿ont /il.z‿ɔ̃/

## 3. Euphonic -t- in inversion
When an inverted verb ends in a vowel (*-a, -e*) or in *-c* (*vainc*) and the subject pronoun is *il, elle, on*, a *-t-* is inserted. See [[Inversion 倒装 inversion|inversion]].
> Parle**-t-**il français ?
> Va**-t-**elle venir ?
> Où **a-t-**on mis les clés ?
> Vainc**-t-**il ?

## 4. Euphonic *l'on*
In formal style, *on* becomes *l'on* after *et, ou, où, si, que*.
> **si l'on** veut, **et l'on** dit, **où l'on** va
> But avoid *l'on* before a word starting with /l/: *si on le lit* (not \*si l'on le lit).

## 5. Special masculine forms of adjectives and determiners
Before a masculine noun beginning with a vowel or *h muet*:
> ce → **cet** homme
> beau → un **bel** arbre
> nouveau → le **nouvel** an
> vieux → un **vieil** ami
> fou → un **fol** espoir, mou → un **mol** oreiller

## 6. Possessive *mon, ton, son* before feminine nouns
> **mon** amie (not \*ma amie), **ton** école, **son** histoire

## 7. Imperative *-s* before *en* and *y*
Verbs whose 2nd-person singular imperative ends in *-e* or *-a* add *-s*:
> Mange → Mange**s**-en !
> Va → Va**s**-y !

## 8. *y* with verb *aller*

With the future and the conditional of *aller*, *y* is omitted for euphonic reasons (to avoid a hiatus).

> – Bruno va-t-il au cours aujourd'hui ? – Oui, il **y va**.

but:

> – Bruno ira-t-il au cours demain ? – Oui, il **ira**. (not \*il y ira)
> J'**irais** volontiers. (not \*j'y irais)

## 9. *Jusque, lorsque, presque, quelque, puisque*
Elision applies to these conjunctions/adverbs in fixed contexts:
> **jusqu'**à, **lorsqu'**il, **puisqu'**on, **quelqu'**un, **presqu'**île

# Remarks

1. Before an *h aspiré*, hiatus is **accepted**: neither elision nor liaison applies. *le héros* /lə.e.ʁo/, *la haine*, *les | haricots*. See [[Elision 省音 élision|elision]].
2. Hiatus is also tolerated before *oui, onze, huit, yaourt*: *le oui*, *le onze*, *le huit*.
3. Linking of vowels across words without pause is called *enchaînement vocalique*; see [[Linking 连音 enchaînement|enchaînement]].
4. Contrast: English avoids hiatus mainly with the article *an* (*an apple*) and linking /r/ in some accents; Chinese has no comparable system, since syllables are separated clearly（如“西安”xī'ān 用隔音符号标出音节界限）.
