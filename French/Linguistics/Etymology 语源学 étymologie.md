# Definition

**Etymology**（语源学，词源学，étymologie）is the study of the origin and history of [[Word 词 mot|words]]: where they come from and how their form and meaning have changed over time. The word "etymology" itself comes from Greek *étymon* "true sense" + *-logía* "study".

The word an etymology traces back to is the **etymon**（词源，étymon）.

> *chef* < Latin ***caput*** "head" → the etymon of *chef* is *caput*

Etymology belongs to historical linguistics and relies on regular **sound changes** (see [[Phonology 音韵学 phonologie|phonology]]) and documented texts, not on resemblance alone.

# The layers of French vocabulary

1. **Popular Latin (inherited)** — words transmitted orally since Roman Gaul, heavily worn down by sound change.
> *aqua* → ***eau***, *augustum* → ***août*** /u(t)/, *calidum* → ***chaud***
2. **Gaulish substrate** — a few dozen words: *chêne*, *alouette*, *ruche*.
3. **Frankish superstrate** (Germanic) — *guerre* (cf. English *war*), *jardin*, *bleu*, *blanc*, and the *h aspiré* in *haine, haut, honte*.
4. **Learned borrowings** from classical Latin and Greek (from the Middle Ages on), close to the original form.
> *fragilis* → popular ***frêle*** / learned ***fragile***
> *potionem* → popular ***poison*** / learned ***potion***
5. **Later borrowings**: Italian (Renaissance: *banque, soldat, balcon*), Arabic (*algèbre, chiffre, magasin*), English (*week-end, sandwich, stress*).

# Etymology and spelling

French [[Orthography 正字法 orthographe|orthography]] is partly etymological: letters are kept (or were added) to show the Latin origin even though they are not pronounced.
> *doigt* (< *digitum*), *temps* (< *tempus*), *poids* (the *d* was added by false association with *pondus*; it actually comes from *pensum*)

The circumflex often marks a lost *s*, which English sometimes retains:
> *forêt* / forest, *hôpital* / hospital, *île* / isle, *fenêtre* (cf. Latin *fenestra*)

# Etymology for learners

Knowing etymology helps link French and English words (see [[Cognate 同源词 cognat|cognate]]) and spot false friends:
> *librairie* 书店 (< *liber* "book") ≠ library (*bibliothèque*)

# Remarks

1. **Folk etymology**（民间词源，étymologie populaire）reshapes words by false association: *choucroute* < Alsatian *sûrkrût* "sour cabbage", reinterpreted as *chou* + *croûte*.
2. 汉语的“词源”研究常与字源（文字学）混在一起；法语中词源与书写形式也有密切关系，但两者仍应区分。
