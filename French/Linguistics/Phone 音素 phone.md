# Definition

A **phone**（音素，单音）is any distinct speech sound, considered purely as a physical/articulatory event, regardless of whether it distinguishes meaning in a given language. Phones are the object of [[Phonetics 语音学 phonétique|phonetics]].

Represented by \[ \].

音素是实际发出来的具体语音。同一个 [[Phoneme 音位 phonème|phoneme]] 在不同环境下可以实现为不同的 phone，这些 phone 称为该音位的 **allophones（音位变体，allophones）**。

# Phone vs phoneme

| | phone 音素 | phoneme 音位 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Level | physical, concrete | abstract, mental |
| Notation | \[ \] (narrow transcription) | / / (broad transcription) |
| Field | [[Phonetics 语音学 phonétique\|phonetics]] | [[Phonology 音韵学 phonologie\|phonology]] |
| Criterion | how it is produced / sounds | whether it distinguishes meaning |

The phone is the **etic** unit and the phoneme the corresponding **emic** unit; see [[Emic units 位单位 unités émiques|Emic units]].

# Examples

English /p/ has (at least) two phones: aspirated \[pʰ\] and unaspirated \[p\].
> **p**in \[pʰɪn\] vs s**p**in \[spɪn\]

French /p/ is never aspirated, which is one reason English speakers' French *Paris* sounds foreign.
> **P**aris \[paʁi\], not \*\[pʰaʁi\]

French /ʁ/ is realized as voiced \[ʁ\] next to voiced sounds but often devoiced \[χ\] after a voiceless consonant or at the end of a word.
> **r**ue \[ʁy\]
> qua**tr**e \[katχ\]

Vowel length in French is allophonic: vowels are lengthened before final \[ʁ, z, ʒ, v\].
> pè**r**e \[pɛːʁ\]
> ro**s**e \[ʁoːz\]

In Mandarin, /a/ surfaces as different phones depending on context: 安 \[an\], 昂 \[ɑŋ\], 烟 \[jɛn\].

# Remarks

1. 中文“音素”一词有时也被用来翻译 phoneme，造成混淆。在本笔记体系中：音素 = phone，[[Phoneme 音位 phonème|音位]] = phoneme。
2. Whether two phones are allophones or separate phonemes depends on the language: \[n\] and \[ŋ\] are separate phonemes in English (*sin* / *sing*) and in Mandarin (安 / 昂), but in French \[ŋ\] occurs only in loanwords (*parking*).
