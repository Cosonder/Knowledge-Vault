# Definition

**Phonetics**（语音学）is the branch of linguistics that studies the physical properties of speech sounds: how they are produced, transmitted and perceived. Its basic unit is the [[Phone 音素 phone|phone]], transcribed in square brackets \[ \] with the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA, *alphabet phonétique international*, API).

语音学研究“声音本身”，不管它在某种语言中是否区别意义；研究“声音在系统中的功能”是 [[Phonology 音韵学 phonologie|phonology]] 的任务。

# Classification

1. **Articulatory phonetics**（发音语音学，phonétique articulatoire）: how the vocal organs produce sounds — place and manner of articulation, voicing, lip rounding, nasality.
2. **Acoustic phonetics**（声学语音学，phonétique acoustique）: the physical sound wave — frequency, formants, duration, intensity.
3. **Auditory / perceptual phonetics**（听觉语音学，phonétique auditive）: how listeners perceive sounds.

# Examples (French)

Describing French sounds articulatorily:
> /y/ in *t**u*** = high front **rounded** vowel（舌位同 /i/，但圆唇）
> /ʁ/ in ***r**ue* = voiced uvular fricative（小舌擦音），vs English alveolar approximant \[ɹ\]
> /ɑ̃/ in *ch**an**t* = open back **nasal** vowel（气流同时从口腔和鼻腔流出）

Phonetic (narrow) vs phonological (broad) transcription:
> *quatre* : /katʁ/ (phonemic) vs \[katχ\] (phonetic: /ʁ/ devoiced after /t/)
> *mère* : /mɛʁ/ vs \[mɛːʁ\] (vowel lengthened before final /ʁ/)

# Remarks

1. French is often described as having a "tense" articulation（发音紧张）: no diphthongization (*beau* \[bo\], not English-like \[boʊ\]), no aspiration, and stress fixed on the last full syllable of the rhythmic group.
2. In the French tradition, *phonétique* is also used loosely for pronunciation teaching in general (*cours de phonétique*).
