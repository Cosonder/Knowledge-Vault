# Definition

**Inflection**（屈折变化，flexion）is the modification of a [[Word 词 mot|word]]'s form to express grammatical categories such as tense, mood, person, number, gender or case. Inflection does not create a new lexeme: *mange, mangeons, mangerait* are all forms of the one verb *manger*. This distinguishes it from **derivation** (*manger* → *mangeable*), which creates a new word. Both are studied in [[Morphology 构词学 morphologie|morphology]].

屈折变化 = 同一个词的不同“语法形式”；派生 = 造出一个新词。词典里只收录屈折变化的“基本形式”（lemma，*forme canonique*），如动词不定式、名词单数、形容词阳性单数。

# Classification

French has two traditional types of inflection:

1. **Conjugation**（变位，conjugaison）— verbal inflection, for [[Person 人称 personne|person]], number, [[Tense 时态 temps|tense]], [[Mood 语气 mode|mood]] and [[Voice 语态 voix|voice]]. See [[Conjugation 变位 conjugaison|conjugation]].
> je **parle**, nous **parlons**, ils **parlaient**, qu'il **parle**, il **parlerait**
2. **Declension**（变格/名词性变化，déclinaison）— nominal inflection: nouns, adjectives, determiners, pronouns.
	* Number: *un chat* → *des chat**s***, *un journal* → *des journ**aux*** (see [[Number of nouns 名词的数 nombre des noms|Number of nouns]])
	* Gender (adjectives, determiners): *petit* → *petit**e***, *le* → ***la*** (see [[Gender of nouns 名词的性 genre des noms|Gender of nouns]])
	* Case survives only in [[Personal pronoun 人称代词 pronom personnel|personal pronouns]] and relative pronouns:
> **il** (subject) / **le** (direct object) / **lui** (indirect object)
> **qui** (subject) / **que** (object) / **dont** (*de* + complement) — see [[Relative pronoun 关系代词 pronom relatif|relative pronoun]]

Inflectional forms are partly driven by [[Agreement 配合 accord|agreement]]: the adjective inflects because the noun it modifies is feminine plural.
> des fleur**s** blanc**hes**

# Means of inflection

* **Suffixation** (most common): *fin-**ir*** → *fin-**issons***
* **Stem alternation**（词干交替）: *je **veu**x / nous **voul**ons*, *je **vais** / nous **all**ons / j'**ir**ai* (suppletion 异干互补)
* **Auxiliary + participle** (periphrastic): *j'**ai mangé***, *je **suis venu*** — see [[Perfect 复合过去时 passé composé|passé composé]]

# Spoken vs written French

Much French inflection is visible only in writing:
> il parle / ils parlent → both /paʁl/
> joli / jolie / jolis / jolies → all /ʒɔli/

Spoken French often marks number on the determiner (*le* /lə/ vs *les* /le/) or through [[Liaison 联诵 liaison|liaison]] (*il aime* /ilɛm/ vs *ils‿aiment* /ilzɛm/).

# Contrast

* English has very little inflection left: *-s, -ed, -ing, -er/-est*, and case in pronouns (*he/him*).
* Mandarin has essentially no inflection; grammatical meanings are expressed by separate words: 了、过、着、们.
* Latin, the ancestor of French, had six cases; French lost them, which is why word order became fixed (SVO).
