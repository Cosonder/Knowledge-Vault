# Definition

A **phoneme**（音位）is the smallest unit of sound that can distinguish meaning in a particular language. It is an abstract category: a set of [[Phone 音素 phone|phones]] (its allophones) that speakers perceive as "the same sound". Phonemes are the object of [[Phonology 音韵学 phonologie|phonology]].

音位是“能区别意义的最小语音单位”。判断两个音是否属于不同音位，标准方法是找 **minimal pair（最小对立对，paire minimale）**：两个词只差一个音，意义就不同。

# Minimal pairs in French

> **p**ain /pɛ̃/ 面包 vs **b**ain /bɛ̃/ 洗澡 → /p/ ≠ /b/
> s**ou** /su/ 苏（硬币） vs s**u** /sy/ 知道（过去分词） → /u/ ≠ /y/
> b**eau** /bo/ 美的 vs b**on** /bɔ̃/ 好的 → oral vs nasal vowel
> poi**ss**on /pwasɔ̃/ 鱼 vs poi**s**on /pwazɔ̃/ 毒药 → /s/ ≠ /z/

# The French phoneme inventory

Standard French has roughly 36 phonemes (the exact count depends on the variety):

* **Oral vowels (12)**: /i e ɛ a ɑ ɔ o u y ø œ ə/. In modern Parisian French /a/–/ɑ/ (*patte* / *pâte*) has largely merged.
* **Nasal vowels (4)**: /ɛ̃ ɑ̃ ɔ̃ œ̃/. Many speakers merge /œ̃/ into /ɛ̃/ (*brun* ≈ *brin*).
* **Semivowels (3)**: /j ɥ w/, as in *pied*, *huit*, *oui*.
* **Consonants (17)**: /p b t d k ɡ f v s z ʃ ʒ m n ɲ l ʁ/ (plus marginal /ŋ/ in loanwords).

# Contrast with English and Chinese

* French has /y/ and /ø œ/ (front rounded vowels), absent from English; Mandarin has /y/ (ü 鱼).
* Aspiration is phonemic in Mandarin（/p/ 拜 vs /pʰ/ 派）but only allophonic in English and absent in French.
* Vowel length is phonemic in English (roughly *ship*/*sheep*, really a quality difference) but not in modern French.
* Tone is phonemic in Mandarin (妈 麻 马 骂) but not in French or English.

# Remarks

1. A phoneme is not a letter. French spelling maps poorly to phonemes: /o/ can be written *o, ô, au, eau*; see [[Orthography 正字法 orthographe|orthography]] and [[French orthography 法语正字法 orthographe française|French orthography]].
2. Phenomena such as [[Liaison 联诵 liaison|liaison]] and [[Elision 省音 élision|elision]] are phonological processes that operate on phonemes at word boundaries.
3. The phoneme is the prototypical [[Emic units 位单位 unités émiques|emic unit]]; the concept gave its name to the whole -eme family (morpheme, grapheme, lexeme…).
