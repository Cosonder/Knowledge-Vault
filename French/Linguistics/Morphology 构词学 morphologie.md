What is morphology?

# Definition

**Morphology**（形态学，词法学，构词学）is the branch of linguistics that studies the internal structure of [[Word 词 mot|words]]: how they are built from smaller meaningful units called **morphemes**（语素，morphèmes）, and how their form varies.

A **morpheme** is the smallest unit that carries meaning or grammatical function. It is the [[Emic units 位单位 unités émiques|emic unit]] of morphology, just as the [[Phoneme 音位 phonème|phoneme]] is for [[Phonology 音韵学 phonologie|phonology]]; its concrete realizations are **morphs**, and variant forms of one morpheme are **allomorphs**（语素变体）.

> *in-croy-able-s* = **in-** (negation) + **croy-** (root of *croire*) + **-able** (adjective "-able") + **-s** (plural)

# Classification

## Types of morphemes
* **Free morpheme**（自由语素）: can stand alone as a word — *table, bien, mer*.
* **Bound morpheme**（黏着语素）: must attach to another — *re-, -ment, -ons*.
* **Root**（词根，radical）vs **affix**（词缀，affixe）: prefix *préfixe*, suffix *suffixe*.
* **Lexical** vs **grammatical** morphemes: *chant-* vs *-ons* in *chantons*.

## Branches of morphology
1. **Inflectional morphology**（屈折形态）: changes the form of a word to express grammatical categories without creating a new word. See [[Inflection 屈折变化 flexion|inflection]].
> *chant**e**, chant**ons**, chant**ai**s* — all forms of the one verb *chanter*
2. **Derivational morphology**（派生，dérivation）: creates a new word, often of a different category.
> *rapide* → *rapide**ment*** (adj. → adv.)
> *faire* → ***dé**faire*, ***re**faire*
> *lire* → *lect**eur*** / *lect**rice***
3. **Compounding**（复合，composition）: combines two or more words.
> *porte-monnaie* 钱包, *pomme de terre* 土豆, *arc-en-ciel* 彩虹
4. Other processes: **conversion**（转类，*le boire, le rire*）, **clipping**（截短，*prof* < *professeur*, *ciné* < *cinéma*）, **acronyms**（*SNCF, ONU*）, **verlan**（*meuf* < *femme*）.

# Language typology

* **Isolating languages**（孤立语）: little or no inflection; grammar via word order and particles. Mandarin is the classic example: 只有“了、着、们”等少数近似屈折的成分，且多为可选。
* **Fusional languages**（屈折语/融合语）: one affix bundles several features. French *-ons* = 1st person + plural + present indicative.
* **Agglutinative languages**（黏着语）: one affix per feature — Turkish, Japanese, Korean.

French is fusional but much less inflected than Latin; English is even less so. In spoken French many written endings are silent, so *chante, chantes, chantent* are all /ʃɑ̃t/.

# Remarks

1. 中文“构词学”严格来说更接近 word formation（派生 + 复合），而 morphology 也包括屈折，所以“形态学”是更全面的译名。
2. Morphology interacts with phonology (morphophonology): *nerveux* /nɛʁvø/ but *nerveuse* /nɛʁvøz/; the feminine reveals a consonant absent in the masculine, which also explains [[Liaison 联诵 liaison|liaison]].
