# Definition

A **phrase**（短语，词组）is a group of one or more [[Word 词 mot|words]] that functions as a single unit in the syntax of a sentence and is organized around a **head**（中心语，tête）. The head determines the category of the phrase: a noun phrase has a noun as its head, a verb phrase a verb, and so on.

In French linguistics the technical term is ***syntagme*** (*syntagme nominal, syntagme verbal*…). *Expression* is used for a fixed or idiomatic phrase (*une expression figée*), which is the subject of [[Phraseology 熟语学 phraséologie|phraseology]].

注意 false friend：French ***phrase*** = English **sentence**（句子），not "phrase".
> Cette **phrase** est trop longue. 这个**句子**太长了。

# Classification

By the category of the head (*catégories syntagmatiques*, see `Syntactic category/Phrasal category`):

1. **Noun phrase (NP)** 名词短语, *syntagme nominal (SN)*
> **le petit chat noir** de ma voisine
2. **Verb phrase (VP)** 动词短语, *syntagme verbal (SV)*
> Il **a donné un livre à Marie**.
3. **Adjective phrase (AP)** 形容词短语, *syntagme adjectival (SA)*
> une fille **très fière de son travail**
4. **Adverb phrase (AdvP)** 副词短语, *syntagme adverbial*
> Il parle **beaucoup trop vite**.
5. **Prepositional phrase (PP)** 介词短语, *syntagme prépositionnel (SP)*
> Je pars **après le dîner**.

# Tests for phrasehood

A phrase behaves as a unit (constituent, *constituant*):
* **Substitution by a pronoun**:
> J'ai vu **le petit chat noir**. → Je **l'**ai vu.
> Je pense **à mon voyage**. → J'**y** pense.
* **Displacement**, e.g. clefting:
> **C'est le petit chat noir** que j'ai vu.
* **Answer to a question**:
> Qu'as-tu vu ? — **Le petit chat noir.**

# Contrast

* Word order inside the NP: French adjectives mostly follow the noun (*un chat **noir***), English adjectives precede (*a **black** cat*); Chinese modifiers always precede, with 的 (一只**黑色的**猫).
* PPs: French and English use prepositions; Chinese uses both prepositions and postpositions（在桌子**上**）.

# Remarks

1. In everyday English "phrase" also means any short expression or idiom ("a common phrase"); in syntax it has the precise meaning above.
2. Grammatical functions (subject, object…) are assigned to phrases, not to single words; see [[Subject 主语 sujet|subject]] and [[Object 宾语 objet|object]].
