# Introduction

A coordinate conjunction (*conjonction de coordination*) links two elements **of the same grammatical function and level**: two words, two phrases, or two independent clauses. Neither element depends on the other, unlike with a [[Subordinate conjunction 从属连词 conjonction de subordination|subordinate conjunction]].

> Paul **et** Marie. (two nouns)
> Il est riche **mais** malheureux. (two adjectives)
> Je pense, **donc** je suis. (two clauses)

# The seven conjunctions

Mnemonic: ***Mais où est donc Ornicar ?*** → *mais, ou, et, donc, or, ni, car*.

| conjunction | meaning                | relation        | example                                                |
| ----------- | ---------------------- | --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| **et**      | and                    | addition        | J'ai acheté du pain **et** du fromage.                 |
| **ou**      | or                     | alternative     | Tu veux du thé **ou** du café ?                        |
| **mais**    | but                    | opposition      | Il est petit **mais** fort.                            |
| **donc**    | so, therefore          | consequence     | Il pleut, **donc** je reste.                           |
| **or**      | now, yet               | transition in reasoning | Tous les hommes sont mortels ; **or** Socrate est un homme. |
| **ni**      | nor                    | negative addition | Il **ne** boit **ni** vin **ni** bière.              |
| **car**     | for, because           | explanation     | Je rentre, **car** il est tard.                        |

注意 *ou*（或者）与 *où*（哪里，关系/疑问词）的区别：只差一个重音符。

# Remarks on usage

## et / ou / ni with repetition

> **Et** toi **et** moi. -> Both you and me.
> **Ou** tu viens, **ou** tu restes. -> Either you come or you stay.
> **Ni** lui **ni** elle **ne** sont venus. -> Neither he nor she came.

*ni* requires *ne* before the verb (see [[Negation 否定 négation|negation]]) and replaces *et / ou* in negative sentences:

> Je n'aime pas le thé **ni** le café. / Je n'aime **ni** le thé **ni** le café.

## car vs parce que

*car* is coordinating: it cannot begin a sentence answering *pourquoi ?* and cannot be preceded by *et* or *c'est*. *parce que* is subordinating.

> Pourquoi pars-tu ? — **Parce que** je suis fatigué. (✗ *Car je suis fatigué.*)

*car* is more written; it gives a justification rather than a direct cause.

## donc

Unlike the others, *donc* can move within the clause (it is often analysed as an adverb):

> Il est **donc** parti. Viens **donc** ! (emphatic)

## or

Not "or": it introduces a new element that changes or advances the argument (*now, however*).

> Il devait venir ; **or** il n'est jamais arrivé.

# Agreement with coordinated subjects

- *et*: plural verb: *Paul **et** Marie **sont** partis.*
- *ou / ni*: plural if both can do the action, singular if they exclude each other: *Paul **ou** Pierre **sera** élu.*

# Other coordinating words

Adverbs of linkage (*adverbes de liaison*) also play a coordinating role: *puis, ensuite, alors, pourtant, cependant, toutefois, en effet, c'est-à-dire, ainsi, aussi* (= therefore, with [[Inversion 倒装 inversion|inversion]]: *Aussi est-il parti*).

# English equivalent

*and, or, but, so, nor, for*; *or* has no direct equivalent (*now, yet*).
