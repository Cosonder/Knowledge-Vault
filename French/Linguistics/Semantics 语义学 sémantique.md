# Definition

**Semantics**（语义学，sémantique）is the branch of linguistics that studies **meaning**: the meaning of [[Word 词 mot|words]] (lexical semantics), of [[Phrase 短语 expression|phrases]] and sentences (compositional semantics), and how meaning relates to the world. The term was coined in French: Michel Bréal, *Essai de sémantique* (1897).

语义学研究“语言表达本身的意义”；研究“说话人在具体语境中想表达的意义”的是 **pragmatics（语用学，pragmatique）**。

> *Tu as l'heure ?* — semantically: "Do you have the time?" (yes/no); pragmatically: a request 请告诉我几点了。

# Classification

1. **Lexical semantics**（词汇语义学）: meaning of words and the relations between them (shared with [[Lexicology 词汇学 lexicologie|lexicology]]).
2. **Compositional / sentential semantics**（组合语义学）: how the meaning of a whole is built from its parts and their syntax.
3. **Formal semantics**: truth conditions, logic, reference.
4. **Cognitive semantics**: prototypes, metaphor, conceptual categories.

# Key notions

* **Sense vs reference**（意义 / 所指，sens / référence）: *l'étoile du matin* and *l'étoile du soir* refer to the same planet (Venus) but have different senses.
* **Denotation vs connotation**（外延 / 内涵）: *maison* vs *baraque* (pejorative, informal).
* **Polysemy vs homonymy**: one word with related senses (*la **tête*** = head / top / leader) vs different words with the same form (***son*** "sound" / ***son*** "his/her" / ***son*** "bran").
* **Compositionality and its limits**: idioms are non-compositional — see [[Phraseology 熟语学 phraséologie|phraseology]].
> *casser **sa pipe*** = 死 (not "break one's pipe")

# French examples

Grammatical meaning is also part of semantics — for example, aspectual differences between tenses:
> Il **lisait** quand je suis arrivé. (ongoing — [[Imperfect 未完成过去时 Imparfait|imparfait]])
> Il **a lu** le livre. (completed — [[Perfect 复合过去时 Passé composé|passé composé]])

Adjective position can change meaning:
> un **grand** homme 伟人 / un homme **grand** 高个子的人
> mon **ancien** professeur 我以前的老师 / un bâtiment **ancien** 古老的建筑
> un **pauvre** homme 可怜的人 / un homme **pauvre** 穷人

Semantic fields cut up reality differently across languages:
> French ***savoir*** / ***connaître*** = English *know*
> English *river* = French ***fleuve*** (flows into the sea) / ***rivière*** (flows into another river)
> Chinese 哥哥 / 弟弟 = French ***frère***
